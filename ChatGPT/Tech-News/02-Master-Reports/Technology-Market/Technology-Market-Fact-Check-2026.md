# Part II --- Positive and negative technology-industry developments (22--31 October 2026)

## 1. Central market theme

AI demand remained strong, but investors increasingly questioned whether
enormous AI infrastructure investments would generate adequate financial
returns.

The market was no longer rewarding every company simply for announcing
AI spending. Investors were looking more closely at revenue growth, free
cash flow, capital expenditure, debt, customer concentration and the
time required for AI projects to become profitable.

The period contained both positive operating results and negative
share-price reactions. A company can report strong revenue growth while
its shares fall because investors expected even better results or became
concerned about future costs.

## 2. Major developments, with positive and negative implications

### 2.1 SK hynix --- strong high-bandwidth memory demand

**Development:** SK hynix reported a sharp increase in quarterly
profitability, with operating profit rising more than sevenfold year
over year. Demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM), used in advanced AI
accelerators, was a major driver.

**Positive implications** - AI accelerators require advanced memory,
creating demand for SK hynix's HBM products. - Strong earnings indicate
that AI infrastructure demand is translating into real sales and profits
for at least some suppliers. - SK hynix is positioned in a strategically
important part of the AI hardware supply chain.

**Negative implications and risks** - Strong current demand can attract
competitors and encourage new manufacturing capacity. - Semiconductor
memory is cyclical; oversupply can reduce prices and margins. -
Dependence on AI-related customers increases exposure to a slowdown in
data-centre investment. - Exceptional year-on-year profit growth can
become harder to repeat as comparisons rise.

**Investor interpretation:** The results support the AI-memory
investment thesis, but investors should monitor memory pricing, HBM
capacity, customer commitments and the wider semiconductor cycle.

### 2.2 Microsoft --- strong fiscal Q1 2027 results, but high AI spending

**Development:** Microsoft reported fiscal Q1 2027 revenue of
approximately **\$92.3 billion**, up **19%** year over year; operating
income of approximately **\$45.9 billion**; and net income of
approximately **\$36.6 billion**.

**Positive implications** - Revenue and profit growth show that
Microsoft's established businesses remain powerful. - Azure and
enterprise cloud demand provide routes to monetise AI. - Microsoft can
distribute AI features through existing enterprise software and cloud
relationships. - Its diversified business reduces dependence on one
product category.

**Negative implications and risks** - AI data centres and infrastructure
require substantial capital expenditure. - Investors need evidence that
AI spending will generate enough incremental revenue and cash flow. -
Cloud competition remains intense. - Workforce restructuring and
voluntary buyout programmes indicate that cost discipline remains
relevant even for a highly profitable company.

**Investor interpretation:** Microsoft has a strong operating
foundation, but its valuation and future returns depend on how
effectively it converts AI investment into recurring revenue and free
cash flow.

### 2.3 Amazon --- accelerating AWS, with cash-flow and spending concerns

**Development:** Amazon's AWS business recorded strong growth, reported
at approximately **39%** in the October reporting period. However,
capital expenditure and cash-flow concerns remained prominent, and the
company's holiday-quarter sales outlook was viewed as weaker than some
expectations.

**Positive implications** - AWS is a major cloud platform and can
benefit as companies move AI workloads into the cloud. - AI
infrastructure demand can increase consumption of compute, storage and
networking services. - Amazon also has retail, logistics and advertising
operations that provide other sources of revenue.

**Negative implications and risks** - Building data centres and buying
equipment consumes substantial capital. - High capital expenditure can
depress free cash flow even while revenue rises. - If AI customers do
not use enough capacity or pay enough for it, returns on infrastructure
investment may disappoint. - A cautious sales outlook can weigh on
sentiment.

**Investor interpretation:** AWS growth is encouraging, but revenue
growth alone is not enough. Monitor free cash flow, capital expenditure
and the return on invested capital.

### 2.4 Alphabet --- pressure on search advertising and AI-related cash outflow

**Development:** Alphabet faced investor disappointment over its core
search advertising performance and a sharp increase in AI-related cash
expenditure. Its share price suffered a record one-day percentage
decline in the reported period.

**Positive implications** - Alphabet has a major advertising business,
YouTube, Google Cloud, Android distribution and substantial AI research
capabilities. - Google Cloud growth and AI products could create new
revenue streams. - Its infrastructure and distribution provide ways to
deploy AI at scale.

**Negative implications and risks** - AI assistants and alternative
search experiences could change how users discover information and how
advertising is sold. - If AI answers reduce traditional search clicks or
ad impressions, the economics of the core business could be pressured. -
Large AI investments can reduce free cash flow before new revenue is
proven. - A strong technology position does not automatically guarantee
successful monetisation.

**Investor interpretation:** Alphabet's central challenge is to make AI
a growth engine without undermining the search advertising business that
funds much of its operations.

### 2.5 Meta --- large fundraising plans and AI investment

**Development:** Meta announced a planned equity fundraising programme
of approximately **\$97.5 billion**, prompting questions about the scale
of AI investment and potential shareholder dilution.

**Positive implications** - Additional capital could support data-centre
expansion and AI infrastructure. - AI tools could improve advertising
targeting, campaign performance and user engagement. - Meta has large
platforms and an established advertising business through Facebook and
Instagram, as well as WhatsApp.

**Negative implications and risks** - Equity issuance can dilute
existing shareholders if new shares are issued. - The fundraising plan
raises questions about how much capital the AI programme requires. -
Investors may become concerned if spending rises faster than the profits
generated by AI. - AI infrastructure can be expensive to build and
operate.

**Investor interpretation:** Meta has a valuable existing business, but
investors need to see measurable returns from the scale of its AI
spending. A fundraising announcement is not the same thing as completed
financing or proven investment returns.

### 2.6 NVIDIA --- strong AI-chip position, but valuation and competition risks

**Development:** NVIDIA remained central to AI infrastructure demand,
while the market increasingly scrutinised the cost and sustainability of
hyperscaler AI investment.

**Positive implications** - Advanced AI systems require substantial
computing capacity. - NVIDIA has an established GPU ecosystem and
software platform. - New generations of accelerators can support
continued demand. - AI adoption across cloud providers and enterprises
could expand the market.

**Negative implications and risks** - AMD and custom chips developed by
large cloud providers can compete for workloads. - Customers' capital
expenditure may slow if AI returns disappoint. - A high valuation can
leave the stock vulnerable even when the company continues growing. -
Semiconductor demand can be cyclical.

**Investor interpretation:** NVIDIA's business position is strong, but
the share-price outcome depends on future growth relative to the
expectations already embedded in its valuation.

### 2.7 Intel --- sharp share-price decline after weak guidance

**Development:** Intel shares fell approximately **29% on 30 October
2026**, its steepest one-day decline since 1974, following disappointing
guidance and concerns about the PC market and costs.

**Positive implications** - Intel retains an opportunity to improve its
manufacturing and foundry position. - A successful manufacturing
turnaround could create long-term strategic value. - Its established
processor business and manufacturing assets provide a base from which to
attempt recovery.

**Negative implications and risks** - Weak PC demand can pressure
revenue. - Manufacturing execution and production yields are difficult
to improve quickly. - Competition from AMD and other chip suppliers
remains intense. - Memory and component cost pressures can affect the
broader PC ecosystem. - A large share-price decline does not prove that
a company is undervalued.

**Investor interpretation:** Intel is a turnaround case. Investors need
evidence of operational improvement, customer wins, manufacturing
progress and sustainable profitability rather than relying on the idea
that the share price has simply fallen enough.

### 2.8 Anthropic --- reported IPO filing

**Development:** Reuters reported that Anthropic had filed for a public
listing process in the period. This was a report of an IPO filing, not
proof that the company had completed an IPO or that public shares were
already trading.

**Positive implications** - A public listing could give the company
access to capital. - Public reporting could provide more visibility into
revenue, costs and profitability. - A listing could create a valuation
benchmark for the frontier-AI industry.

**Negative implications and risks** - Advanced AI models are expensive
to train and operate. - Competition can put pressure on prices and
margins. - Public investors may demand a clear path to profitability. -
An IPO can be priced at a valuation that already assumes very high
future growth.

**Investor interpretation:** Distinguish a filing from a completed
listing. Private-market access and public-market shares have different
liquidity, disclosure and risk characteristics.

### 2.9 Broadcom and Anthropic --- reported \$63 billion financing arrangement

**Development:** The Associated Press reported a **\$63 billion
financing arrangement** connected to Anthropic's access to Broadcom AI
accelerators and networking infrastructure.

**Positive implications** - The arrangement highlights demand for AI
accelerators and high-speed networking. - Broadcom may benefit from
supplying custom silicon and data-centre networking. - AI companies need
more than GPUs; they also need the network infrastructure to connect
large computing systems.

**Negative implications and risks** - Large financing arrangements
create execution and counterparty risks. - Dependence on a small number
of large AI customers can create concentration risk. - The economics of
the arrangement matter: access to infrastructure does not automatically
mean that the AI customer will generate sufficient returns. - Announced
financing or access arrangements should not be confused with completed
cash investment or guaranteed profit.

**Investor interpretation:** The deal illustrates the scale of AI
infrastructure commitments, but investors should assess the commercial
terms, delivery milestones and customer economics.

### 2.10 Vertiv --- stronger outlook from data-centre infrastructure demand

**Development:** Vertiv raised full-year revenue and profit forecasts
after strong quarterly performance. Its shares rose approximately
**13.2%** in the reported reaction.

**Positive implications** - AI data centres require power management and
advanced cooling. - Higher rack density and more demanding compute
workloads can increase demand for cooling systems. - The company can
benefit from both new data-centre construction and upgrades to existing
facilities. - The results show that AI investment can create
opportunities beyond semiconductor manufacturers.

**Negative implications and risks** - Vertiv depends on continued
data-centre construction and capital spending. - Project delays, power
availability and customer budget changes can affect orders. -
Competition and execution can affect margins. - A strong share-price
reaction can raise valuation risk.

**Investor interpretation:** Vertiv offers exposure to the physical
infrastructure behind AI, but the share price must be assessed against
future orders, margins, backlog conversion and valuation.

### 2.11 US technology-market selloff on 30 October 2026

**Development:** Major US equity indices fell sharply on Friday, 30
October, as concerns about AI valuations, infrastructure spending and
cash burn intensified.

Reported closing figures: - **S&P 500:** −1.82%, closing at
approximately **7,580.07**. - **Nasdaq Composite:** −2.86%
(approximately −2.9%), closing at approximately **25,437**. - **Dow
Jones Industrial Average:** −1.09% (approximately −1.1%), closing at
approximately **51,770.31**. - **Russell 2000:** −1.7%, closing at
approximately **2,811.84**. - Intel fell approximately 29%.

For the week, the S&P 500, Dow and Nasdaq lost approximately 0.6%, 0.9%
and 2.0%, respectively, while the Russell 2000 gained approximately
1.3%.

Despite the declines, the cited AP report stated that all four indices
remained positive for the year: - S&P 500: +10.7%. - Dow: +7.7%. -
Nasdaq: +9.4%. - Russell 2000: +13.3%.

**Positive implications** - A market correction can reduce excessive
valuations and make future entry prices more reasonable. - The selloff
did not erase the indices' year-to-date gains in the cited report. -
Investors may become more disciplined about distinguishing companies
with real cash generation from companies relying primarily on future
promises.

**Negative implications and risks** - Broad technology selling can
affect companies even when their individual operating results remain
strong. - High valuations make shares sensitive to disappointing
guidance. - Concerns about debt, cash burn and AI capital expenditure
can spread across the technology sector. - A correction can become more
severe if spending plans are cut or earnings disappoint.

**Investor interpretation:** A market decline is not automatically a
buying opportunity. The investor must determine whether the decline
reflects temporary sentiment or a genuine deterioration in future
earnings and cash flows.

## 1. How to read these projections

The following is a qualitative scenario for someone investing around the
October 2026 market level and holding over time. It assumes no
additional contributions. It does not calculate exact share prices,
annual percentage returns or a final portfolio value.

The future cannot be known with precision. Actual returns depend on: -
The entry valuation and purchase price. - Revenue and earnings growth. -
Profit margins and free cash flow. - Capital expenditure and debt. -
Share issuance and dilution. - Dividends and reinvestment. - Competition
and technological change. - Currency movements and taxes for an investor
whose home currency is not the US dollar.

A good company can be a poor investment if purchased at too high a
price. A share price can also fall even while the underlying company
grows, if investors reduce the valuation multiple they are willing to
pay.

## 4. Three possible market scenarios through 2031

### Scenario A --- Optimistic: AI becomes a major source of business productivity

-   Businesses pay for AI services and generate measurable savings.
-   Cloud providers recover their infrastructure investments.
-   Demand for GPUs, HBM memory, networking, power and cooling remains
    high.
-   AI tools become embedded in normal business operations.
-   Investors in successful companies could see substantial long-term
    gains.

Likely beneficiaries include companies that can convert AI demand into
durable profits: cloud platforms, chip suppliers, memory manufacturers,
networking providers and data-centre infrastructure companies.

### Scenario B --- Base case: AI adoption continues, but growth normalises

-   Some AI companies become profitable; others struggle.
-   Semiconductor demand grows but experiences periodic corrections.
-   Infrastructure companies benefit, although not all at the same rate.
-   Investors make money selectively rather than from buying every
    AI-related stock.
-   Valuation and cash-flow discipline become more important.

This scenario does not require an AI collapse. It simply assumes that
the industry matures and that not every investment produces exceptional
returns.

### Scenario C --- Pessimistic: AI investment grows faster than the revenue it generates

-   Data-centre projects are delayed or cancelled.
-   Cloud companies reduce spending.
-   Chip suppliers face falling orders and excess inventory.
-   Highly valued technology stocks suffer major corrections.
-   Companies with heavy debt, weak cash flow or poor profitability face
    the greatest pressure.

This scenario is not imaginary: the cited industry outlook identified
delayed returns, power shortages and potential project cancellations as
risks to the 2027--2028 semiconductor outlook.

## 5. Overall investment judgement

### Established businesses with multiple revenue sources

**Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet and Meta**

These companies have existing businesses that can help finance their AI
ambitions. Their central question is whether AI investment adds enough
profit to justify the spending.

### Companies selling essential AI infrastructure

**NVIDIA, Broadcom, SK hynix and Vertiv**

These companies could benefit from AI infrastructure expansion, but
their risks differ: - NVIDIA: chip competition, valuation and customer
spending. - Broadcom: customer concentration and custom-chip
execution. - SK hynix: memory pricing and supply cycles. - Vertiv:
data-centre construction, power availability and project timing.

### Higher-risk opportunities

**Intel and Anthropic**

-   Intel depends on a successful operational turnaround.
-   Anthropic depends on successful commercialisation and a sensible
    entry valuation, especially if it becomes publicly listed.

**AMD** sits between the established infrastructure suppliers and
higher-risk growth opportunities: it has an established processor
business but must prove that it can capture and sustain a meaningful
share of AI accelerator demand.

## 6. The most important investment lesson: a good company is not always a good investment

Example:

-   An investor puts \$1,000 into a company.
-   The company's business doubles in size over five years.
-   But investors become less willing to pay a premium for its shares.
-   The stock might rise much less than the business, remain flat, or
    even fall.

This is why investment decisions should not be based only on which
companies are making the most exciting technological progress.

Investors should examine:

1.  Revenue growth.
2.  Operating profit and profit margins.
3.  Free cash flow after capital expenditure.
4.  Debt and shareholder dilution.
5.  Price-to-earnings ratio and valuation relative to future growth.
6.  Whether customers are actually making money from the technology they
    purchase.

# Final conclusion

The October 2026 technology market presented a mixed picture. AI demand
remained a powerful source of growth, especially for semiconductors,
memory, cloud computing, networking and data-centre infrastructure. At
the same time, investors became more concerned about the enormous cost
of building AI capacity and the time required to convert that spending
into sustainable profits.

The fact-check found the original Grok report broadly directionally
useful but numerically imperfect, with an approximate 80% qualitative
accuracy assessment and specific corrections to market capitalisations
and performance descriptions.

The positive-and-negative event review showed that strong operating
performance does not guarantee a rising share price. Investors are
increasingly demanding evidence of cash generation, sustainable margins
and returns on capital.

The 2027--2031 outlook suggests that the strongest long-term outcomes
are likely to come from companies that combine durable competitive
advantages with profitable AI monetisation. The greatest risks sit with
businesses whose valuations assume flawless execution, whose capital
expenditure outruns cash generation, or whose future depends on a
difficult turnaround.
