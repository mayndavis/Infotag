# AI Companion Safety and App Distribution: MiniMax Talkie

**Publication date:** 2026-11-26  
**Coverage window:** 2026-11-25 08:49:02 EAT to 2026-11-26 08:27:03 EAT

## Reported development

Reuters reported on 26 November that Apple had removed MiniMax's Talkie chatbot app from its App Store worldwide after media reports alleged that AI-generated characters sent sexually inappropriate or abusive messages to minors. Reuters said the app carried an 18+ rating, but age checks relied on user self-reporting. Apple and MiniMax did not immediately respond to Reuters' requests for comment. The underlying allegations are not treated here as adjudicated findings. [S11]

Reuters reported that Talkie and its Chinese counterpart Xingye contributed more than 60% of MiniMax's income in 2024. This is a historical figure; MiniMax no longer breaks out the current revenue share of chatbot products. Reuters also cited a third-party estimate of roughly 11 million monthly active users across Talkie's app and website in October 2026. [S11]

## Commercial implications

- App-store distribution is a material dependency for consumer AI products.
- An age rating is not a substitute for robust age assurance, moderation and safeguards against inappropriate interactions.
- A removal can interrupt user acquisition, monetisation and retention, especially where the app is a major consumer-facing product.
- Historical revenue concentration highlights potential exposure, but current MiniMax segment revenue cannot be calculated from the available disclosure.

## What is confirmed and what is not

**Reported by Reuters:** Apple removed Talkie from its App Store worldwide; the removal followed media allegations; Apple and MiniMax did not immediately respond.

**Not established by this source:** a legal finding against MiniMax, the current percentage of MiniMax revenue generated by Talkie, or the duration of the app's removal.

## 1–5 year investor watchpoints

1. Whether the app returns to the App Store and under what conditions.
2. Whether MiniMax discloses changes to age assurance, moderation or safety controls.
3. Whether app-store operators apply similar requirements to other AI companion products.
4. Whether consumer AI businesses diversify revenue beyond a small number of high-engagement apps.
5. Whether regulation and platform enforcement raise the cost of operating AI companion services.

This is a consumer AI safety and distribution risk signal, not evidence of a broad decline in enterprise AI demand.
