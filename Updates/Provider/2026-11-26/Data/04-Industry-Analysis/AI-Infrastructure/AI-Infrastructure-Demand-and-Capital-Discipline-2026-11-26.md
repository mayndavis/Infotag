# AI Infrastructure Demand and Capital Discipline

**Publication date:** 2026-11-26  
**Coverage window:** 2026-11-25 08:49:02 EAT to 2026-11-26 08:27:03 EAT

## 1. Market repricing is testing returns, not just demand

Reuters reported a sharp intraday sell-off in AI-linked equities on 25 November, followed by a partial recovery. The Nasdaq-100 briefly approached a 20% drawdown from its October record but closed down 0.6%. NVIDIA closed down 3.9%; Alphabet closed down 2.1%; Broadcom recovered to finish roughly flat. CoreWeave's 2031 bond yield approached 18%, according to Tradeweb data cited by Reuters. [S4]

Reuters linked the market pressure to several factors, including the cost of AI infrastructure, debt-funded expansion, uncertainty over cash returns, proposed tariffs and inflation data. It did not attribute the entire market move to one cause. [S4]

**Interpretation:** the market is increasingly distinguishing between the existence of AI demand and the returns earned on the capital used to serve it. A company can have strong orders and still disappoint investors if financing costs, depreciation, power expense or utilisation make returns inadequate.

## 2. Australia: official data shows a sharp rise in data-centre-related equipment spending

The Australian Bureau of Statistics reported that private new capital expenditure, in seasonally adjusted chain-volume terms, rose 12.2% in the September quarter of 2026 and was 18.6% higher year on year. Equipment, plant and machinery rose 23.1% quarter on quarter. [S12, S13]

The information media and telecommunications industry recorded an 86.7% quarterly increase to a record level. Within that industry, the equipment and machinery component rose 183.5% quarter on quarter and 206.7% year on year. ABS said the increase was driven in part by businesses investing in data-centre equipment, including server racks and processing equipment. The industry category is broader than data centres and must not be treated as a pure data-centre spending total. [S12, S13]

The ABS's Estimate 4 for 2026–27 was A$223.7 billion, 11.8% above Estimate 3. This is an expenditure estimate based on business expectations and partial actual data, not completed future investment. [S13]

## 3. Air cargo: server racks and semiconductors remain important shipment drivers

SATS CEO Kerry Mok told Reuters that server racks and semiconductors had been major drivers of air-cargo growth over the previous year and that AI-related demand remained robust during the peak season. SATS cargo volume rose 8% in July–September, compared with 5.3% global growth in the IATA benchmark cited by Reuters. SATS said cargo growth had outpaced industry growth for the 12th consecutive quarter. [S7]

SATS's own half-year FY2027 results show why volume growth must be separated from profitability: revenue rose 10.1% to S$3.389 billion, but operating profit declined 1.2% to S$279.2 million and operating margin fell to 8.2% from 9.2%. Net cash from operating activities fell to S$265.0 million from S$356.8 million, mainly due to higher working-capital outflows. SATS is a diversified aviation-services company, not a pure AI infrastructure business. [S8]

## 4. Malaysia and Southeast Asia: export-control exposure in the project pipeline

MIDA reported RM123.5 billion of approved data-centre and cloud-computing investments across 86 projects in the first nine months of 2026, up 44.5% year on year. These represented 35.2% of all approved investments. MIDA explains that approved investments are proposed projects granted relevant approvals and that implementation commonly takes 18–24 months. The figure is not cash already deployed or operating capacity. [S10]

The Straits Times updated its Malaysia data-centre coverage on 26 November, describing possible exposure for facilities reliant on Chinese customers seeking access to advanced US chips. It also reported that facilities with diversified customer bases and broader colocation or cloud services may be less exposed. [S9]

One anonymous industry source told The Straits Times that up to half of signed future capacity in Southeast Asia might not proceed. This is a single-source estimate, not a verified cancellation rate or official forecast. It is not used as the base case. [S9]

**Investor implications**
- Customer mix and licensing status matter as much as the headline megawatt pipeline.
- Projects with diversified customers and non-AI workloads may be more resilient than facilities dependent on restricted advanced-compute demand.
- Land, grid reservations, power contracts and construction commitments can become stranded or underutilised if expected customers cannot legally access the capacity.
- Approved projects, signed capacity, construction starts, energised capacity and paying utilisation are distinct milestones.

## 5. 1–5 year investor framework

Monitor:
1. Actual server shipments and commissioned capacity, not only announcements.
2. Utilisation and contracted revenue per megawatt.
3. Power availability, grid connection dates, cooling efficiency and energy cost.
4. Financing costs, debt maturity profiles, lease obligations and customer credit quality.
5. Operating cash flow after working capital and capital expenditure.
6. Customer concentration and export-control exposure.
7. Return on invested capital after depreciation, financing and power costs.

**Bottom line:** official capex and cargo evidence support continued physical AI infrastructure deployment. The market sell-off indicates that investors are becoming more demanding about who funds that deployment and whether the resulting assets earn an adequate return.
