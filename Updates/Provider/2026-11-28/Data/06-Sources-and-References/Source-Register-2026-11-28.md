## Source-quality notes

- S1 is the only investigative account in this register. It relies on three people familiar with the matter for the breach itself; all five sources cited in its operational-effects section spoke anonymously.
- S1 says Reuters could not establish the attackers' identity, the full scope of the intrusion or whether data had been stolen. The specific vulnerability was not identified.
- S1 attributes the control-plane access allegation to one source. It does not establish whether the relevant control plane belonged to Cloud One's shared management layer, a contractor, or a commercial cloud provider.
- S1 describes Cloud One as combining services from Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet. It does not identify which provider or environment was affected.
- S2 independently sought comment from the Air Force and Department of Defense. Its breach narrative relies on Reuters, so S1 and S2 must not be counted as two independent confirmations.
- The Air Force statements quoted by Reuters and CBS do not confirm or deny the specific incident. The Department of Defense statement quoted by CBS is a general assurance, not an incident-specific technical assessment.
- The Guardian's 27 November article was opened but excluded from the unique source count because it explicitly relied on Reuters and added no independent confirmation.
- No public Air Force incident notice, technical post-mortem, named vulnerability identifier, provider-specific disclosure or verified data-exfiltration finding was located in the reviewed material.
- No financial impact, contract loss, remediation cost or valuation effect is quantified.

No 100% verification claim is asserted. The incident is classified as **reported and not officially confirmed**.
