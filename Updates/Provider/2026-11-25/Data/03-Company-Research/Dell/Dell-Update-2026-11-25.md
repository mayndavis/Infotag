## 1. Confirmed results

Dell reported record fiscal Q3 2027 revenue of $54.937 billion, up 103% year over year. GAAP diluted EPS was $8.82, up 287%, and non-GAAP diluted EPS was $9.45, up 265%. [S1, S2]

|---|---:|---:|
| Total revenue | $54.937B | +103% |
| Infrastructure Solutions Group revenue | $41.974B | +198% |
| AI-Optimized Servers revenue | $22.031B | +291% |
| Traditional Servers and Networking revenue | $15.013B | +235% |
| Storage revenue | $4.930B | +24% |
| Cash flow from operations | $4.459B | +280% |
| Free cash flow, company non-GAAP measure | $3.474B | +587% |

Dell said it booked $60.2 billion of AI-server orders during the quarter and ended the quarter with a record $132.3 billion AI backlog. It said more than 7,500 customers were deploying Dell AI Factories. Management stated that orders were vetted for customer quality and access to capital, land and power. [S1, S2]

## 3. Cash conversion

Dell reported $4.459 billion of operating cash flow and $985 million of capital expenditures plus capitalised software development costs in Q3. Its stated free cash flow measure was $3.474 billion. For the first nine months of FY27, it reported $10.765 billion operating cash flow, $3.187 billion capital expenditures and capitalised software costs, and $7.578 billion free cash flow. [S2]

Dell's adjusted free cash flow was $4.288 billion for the quarter and $15.602 billion year to date. The adjusted figure includes additional adjustments for financing receivables and equipment under operating leases. It is not interchangeable with free cash flow or GAAP operating cash flow. [S2]

Dell returned $4.9 billion to shareholders during Q3 and approved a further $25 billion share-repurchase authorisation. The authorisation is not evidence that the entire amount has already been repurchased. [S1, S2]

## 4. Investor implications

**Positive**

- The AI infrastructure cycle is producing large recognised server revenue, not just announced projects.
- The guidance increase indicates management expects the current demand and delivery environment to continue through FY27.
- Reported free cash flow provides evidence that the growth is generating cash, although the adjusted and unadjusted measures must remain distinct.

**Risks**

- AI backlog is not guaranteed revenue. Conversion depends on components, customer financing, land, power, delivery and acceptance.
- AI-server order intake of $60.2 billion was approximately 2.7 times quarterly AI-server revenue of $22.031 billion. This is derived arithmetic, not a company-reported backlog-conversion ratio.
- Reuters reported that Dell expects memory-chip shortages to continue into 2027. That could constrain deliveries and increase input-cost or working-capital pressure. [S3]
- Large order volumes can increase customer concentration and execution risk. The public release does not provide enough detail to quantify these exposures.

## 5. NVIDIA and supplier read-through

Reuters described Dell's AI servers as powered by NVIDIA chips. This makes Dell's results relevant to NVIDIA's demand environment, but the disclosure does not quantify NVIDIA's share of Dell's purchases or establish a specific NVIDIA order value. [S3]

The memory-supply warning is relevant to the wider DRAM/HBM supply chain, including SK hynix, but it is not SK hynix company guidance and should not be used as a direct forecast for its revenue or margins.

## 6. 1–5 year outlook

- **0–12 months:** Monitor backlog conversion, component supply, Q4 guidance delivery and working-capital needs.
- **1–3 years:** Track whether AI-server growth broadens beyond a small group of hyperscalers and whether margins hold as system complexity and memory costs rise.
- **3–5 years:** The central question is whether infrastructure demand remains durable after the first buildout wave and whether the resulting cash flows justify the capital and inventory required.
