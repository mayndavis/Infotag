## 2. Data-centre power: capacity growth and grid demand

Reuters reported that Goldman Sachs expects US data-centre power growth to remain strong through 2028. The brokerage said an additional 52 GW of data-centre power capacity could come online in 2028. Goldman also said year-to-date data-centre power capacity and demand had increased by nearly 30%, contributing more than one-third of US total power-demand growth. [S4]

Goldman attributed the growth to more facilities, larger physical footprints and higher power density. It said the expansion could tighten US power markets and increase natural-gas demand, while policy shifts following the US midterm elections could moderate the upside risk. These are Goldman estimates and interpretations as relayed by Reuters. The underlying Goldman research note was not directly retrieved.

### Investor implications

**Potential beneficiaries:** suppliers of power generation, grid equipment, electrical distribution, transformers, switchgear, backup systems, cooling, and data-centre construction may benefit if capacity is actually funded, permitted, connected and energised.

**Potential constraints:** power procurement, transmission upgrades, gas supply, permitting, local opposition, water availability and equipment lead times can delay projects. Announced megawatts are not equivalent to energised capacity or paying workloads.

**Capital intensity:** data-centre developers and hyperscalers must fund power infrastructure before the full revenue contribution is realised. Delays can leave capital tied up while interest, lease, depreciation and operating costs continue.

**1–5 year view:** power availability and time-to-energisation are likely to remain critical differentiators. Investors should monitor capacity that has reached energisation, utilisation, power-purchase costs, grid connection milestones and the economics of behind-the-meter generation rather than relying solely on announced campus capacity.

## 3. AI infrastructure credit risk: the cost of funding is becoming more visible

Reuters reported that five-year CDS spreads for Oracle, Alphabet, SpaceX and CoreWeave reached record levels overnight. It reported the following market observations: [S5]

- SoftBank's five-year CDS spread reached 501 basis points, its widest since October 2022.
- Oracle's spread reached a record 356 basis points. Reuters explained that this corresponds to $3.56 to insure $100 of Oracle debt.
- CoreWeave's spread reached 1,314 basis points. Reuters cited an S&P Global Market Intelligence estimate of a 68% probability of default over five years.
- Alphabet was among the issuers whose five-year CDS spreads reached record levels, but the Reuters article did not provide an exact Alphabet spread in the cited passage.

CDS spreads are the price of credit protection. Wider spreads indicate that investors demand more compensation to insure against credit risk. They do not mean that an issuer has defaulted, and a model-implied default probability is not a prediction that default will occur.

Reuters reported that investors are concerned that returns on debt-funded AI infrastructure investment may take too long to justify the borrowing. It also quoted market participants concerned about circular financing and off-balance-sheet commitments. The article noted that earnings remain supportive and that near-term default risk is low for large-revenue companies such as Alphabet. [S5]

### Investor interpretation

**Positive:** Credit-market repricing can discipline marginal projects and encourage more careful underwriting of power, customer credit, utilisation and contracted cash flows. Strong-balance-sheet hyperscalers may retain better access to funding than smaller infrastructure operators.

**Negative:** Higher debt spreads raise the cost of financing for issuers that need external capital to build data centres. If financing costs rise while projects are delayed or underutilised, interest expense and lease obligations can erode project returns. A slowdown in credit availability could affect suppliers even before end-user demand materially declines.

**What this does not prove:** The CDS observations do not prove that Alphabet, Oracle or other hyperscalers are insolvent, that AI demand has collapsed, or that announced capex has been cancelled. They indicate that investors are charging more for some forms of AI-related credit exposure.

**1–5 year view:** The quality of AI infrastructure returns will depend on the relationship between financing cost, utilisation, customer concentration, depreciation, power cost and recurring revenue. Investors should distinguish investment-grade hyperscalers with diversified cash flows from more leveraged infrastructure specialists.

## 4. Water-use reporting: a methodological caution

Reuters reported a new Enverus Intelligence Research comparison that estimated $25 of monthly AI spending at about 1.9 gallons of water consumption, including water used to generate electricity. The same report compared that spending benchmark with almond milk and golf-course green fees and attributed about 90% of AI-related water consumption in its model to power plants rather than data centres. [S6]

The underlying Enverus report was not retrieved directly during this scan. The comparison is therefore recorded as a secondary-source research claim, not as an independently validated measurement.

The spending-normalised comparison cannot by itself establish:

- total water consumed by the AI sector;
- water consumption per model, token, inference or training run;
- local water stress at a specific data-centre site;
- the difference between water withdrawal and water consumption;
- whether a facility uses evaporative, liquid, immersion or closed-loop cooling;
- whether water use is shifted to electricity generation in a water-stressed region.

It should not be used to dismiss local water constraints. A low water footprint per dollar of consumer spending can coexist with significant absolute consumption or local pressure at a specific site.

## 6. What investors should monitor next

### Next 0–12 months

- Whether Dell converts its $132.3 billion AI backlog into recognised revenue without material cancellations or delivery delays.
- Whether memory availability and component pricing constrain server shipments or margins.
- Whether data-centre power projects secure permits, grid connections and actual energisation.
- Whether CDS spreads stabilise or continue widening for leveraged AI infrastructure issuers.
- Whether hyperscalers revise capital expenditure, capacity plans or financing structures.

### 1–3 years

- Utilisation and monetisation of installed AI compute capacity.
- Revenue and cash flow per megawatt of energised capacity.
- Power costs, cooling efficiency, networking bottlenecks and equipment depreciation.
- Customer concentration and credit quality at neoclouds and infrastructure developers.
- Whether supplier backlogs convert into cash at acceptable working-capital and margin levels.

### 3–5 years

- Return on incremental invested capital across the AI infrastructure stack.
- Whether the growth in AI workloads sustains enough recurring revenue to service the debt and leases used to build capacity.
- Whether power supply, grid upgrades and cooling technology keep pace with compute demand.
- Whether custom silicon and alternative accelerators reduce the economics of dependence on any single supplier.

## 7. Valuation and investment caution

No price target or buy/sell recommendation is provided. No current valuation multiple is asserted because a timestamp-aligned market-capitalisation and earnings dataset was not assembled for this scan.

Dell's results are a strong operating signal, but a strong quarter does not automatically establish that the shares are attractively valued. Likewise, wider CDS spreads indicate increased credit-risk pricing, not proof that a company will default.

The most important distinction is between **demand evidence** (orders, backlog and customer deployments), **delivery evidence** (recognised revenue and installed capacity), and **return evidence** (free cash flow, utilisation and ROIC). These should not be treated as interchangeable.
