# AI Infrastructure: Capital, Power and Credit Risk

**Publication date:** 2026-11-25  
**Coverage window:** 2026-11-24 19:01:00 EAT to 2026-11-25 08:49:02 EAT  
**Evidence classes:** Company-reported results; analyst estimates reported by Reuters; credit-market observations reported by Reuters; secondary-source research context.

---

## 1. Demand is visible, but backlog is not cash

Dell's fiscal Q3 2027 results provide a concrete operating datapoint for AI infrastructure demand. Dell reported $22.031 billion of AI-Optimized Server revenue, up 291% year over year, and $60.2 billion of new AI-server orders. Its ending AI backlog was $132.3 billion. [S1, S2]

This supports the view that AI infrastructure investment is translating into supplier revenue. It does not prove that every order will ship on schedule or that all projects will earn attractive returns.

Dell management said orders were vetted for customer quality and access to capital, land and power. Those checks highlight the dependencies that sit between an order and a completed, revenue-producing AI data centre.

## 2. Power growth is a material constraint

Reuters reported that Goldman Sachs expects an additional 52 GW of US data-centre power capacity could come online in 2028. Goldman also estimated that year-to-date data-centre power capacity and demand had increased nearly 30%, contributing more than one-third of total US power-demand growth. [S4]

These figures are analyst estimates relayed by Reuters, not realised capacity. The same Reuters report said Goldman expects the buildout to support higher electricity and natural-gas demand, while policy changes could moderate the outlook.

### Investment implications

- Grid connections, generation, transformers, switchgear, electrical distribution, backup systems and cooling can become bottlenecks.
- A project that has land and financing but lacks firm power cannot generate the planned compute revenue.
- Behind-the-meter generation can accelerate energisation but adds fuel, permitting, emissions, maintenance and financing considerations.
- Announced capacity should be tracked separately from capacity under construction, connected to the grid, energised and productively utilised.

## 3. Credit markets are scrutinising AI infrastructure financing

Reuters reported record five-year CDS spreads for Oracle, Alphabet, SpaceX and CoreWeave. It reported SoftBank at 501 basis points, Oracle at 356 basis points and CoreWeave at 1,314 basis points. The article cited a 68% five-year default-probability estimate for CoreWeave from S&P Global Market Intelligence. [S5]

These are market observations and model-derived risk estimates. They are not defaults or certain predictions. Reuters also reported that earnings remain supportive and near-term default risk is low for large-revenue companies such as Alphabet.

The concern is that the debt, leases and other commitments used to finance AI infrastructure may mature or require servicing before the capacity generates enough recurring cash flow. Circular financing and off-balance-sheet commitments can make it harder for investors to assess the ultimate economic exposure.

### What to monitor

- debt maturities, interest costs and refinancing spreads;
- lease commitments and guarantees;
- contracted versus speculative capacity;
- customer credit quality and concentration;
- energisation milestones and utilisation;
- cash flow after power, depreciation and financing costs;
- return on incremental invested capital.

## 4. Water-use claims require careful interpretation

Reuters reported an Enverus Intelligence Research comparison that normalised water consumption by monthly consumer spending. It estimated $25 of AI spending at about 1.9 gallons, including water used to generate electricity. [S6]

The underlying report was not retrieved directly. The comparison is not treated as a definitive measure of absolute AI-sector water use, local water stress or cooling efficiency. Spending-normalised comparisons can provide context but cannot replace facility-level data on withdrawals, consumption, cooling design, water source and watershed conditions.

## 5. Investor horizon

- **0–12 months:** backlog conversion, component availability, project funding, power procurement and credit spreads.
- **1–3 years:** energised megawatts, utilisation, power cost, debt servicing and customer concentration.
- **3–5 years:** whether AI workload revenue and productivity gains generate returns above the cost of capital across the hardware, cloud, power and cooling stack.

**Core conclusion:** The AI infrastructure opportunity is real enough to show up in supplier revenue, but the economics depend on more than chip demand. Power, financing, delivery, utilisation and cash conversion determine whether growth creates durable shareholder value.
