# South Korea Semiconductor Export Outlook

**Publication date:** 2026-11-27  
**Coverage window:** 2026-11-26 08:54:41 EAT to 2026-11-27 08:23:18 EAT

## 1. New November export forecast

Reuters reported on 27 November at 05:03 UTC that a poll of eight economists produced a median forecast for South Korean November exports of **96.8% year-on-year growth**, up from 93.9% in October. If realised, Reuters said this would be the fastest rate since January 1974 and the 18th consecutive month of export growth.

The same poll forecast:
- imports rising 38.9% year on year;
- a monthly trade surplus of US$48.03 billion.

These are economist survey estimates, not official November trade results. South Korea's November trade figures are scheduled for release on 1 December 2026. [S1]

## 2. Official preliminary data for the first 20 days

The Korea Customs Service published preliminary customs-clearance data on 23 November for 1–20 November:
- total exports: **US$77.4 billion**, up 101.5% year on year;
- semiconductor exports: **US$38.466 billion**, up 294.4%;
- semiconductor share of total exports: **49.7%**;
- total imports: US$50.288 billion, up 39.4%;
- trade surplus: US$27.112 billion.

These are preliminary figures for the first 20 days, not final full-month exports. [S2]

## 3. What this means for the semiconductor cycle

The export data are consistent with exceptionally strong demand for Korean semiconductor products during the AI infrastructure build-out. They are relevant to memory suppliers including SK hynix and Samsung Electronics, but they are not company earnings data.

The public figures do not isolate:
- SK hynix's share of exports;
- Samsung's share;
- HBM versus conventional DRAM or NAND;
- export volumes versus price effects;
- customer concentration;
- gross margin, capex or free cash flow.

It would be incorrect to convert the aggregate export growth rate into a specific SK hynix revenue or earnings forecast without company-level shipment, pricing and product-mix evidence.

## 4. Investor implications

**Positive indicators**
- Strong export values indicate robust demand and pricing conditions across the Korean semiconductor export base.
- Memory suppliers with qualified high-bandwidth-memory products and sufficient yields may benefit from continued AI-related demand.
- The data support continued monitoring of HBM capacity, customer qualification and memory supply constraints.

**Risks**
- Semiconductor concentration makes Korean exports more sensitive to a reversal in AI infrastructure spending or a memory price-cycle downturn.
- Export value can rise because of price, volume or product mix; the customs figures alone do not separate these effects.
- Supply expansion requires substantial capex and can pressure returns if demand normalises before new capacity is absorbed.
- Trade restrictions and customer concentration can affect which products can be shipped and where they can be used.

## 5. What to monitor next

1. Official November trade figures on 1 December 2026.
2. SK hynix and Samsung reported HBM/DRAM shipments, pricing, yields and customer qualification.
3. HBM capacity additions and capex commitments.
4. Inventory levels and customer order patterns.
5. Operating cash flow and returns on incremental invested capital.

**Assessment:** the latest trade indicators support a strong semiconductor demand environment, but they do not prove that every memory producer will earn the same growth or margin. Company-level conclusions require company-level evidence.
