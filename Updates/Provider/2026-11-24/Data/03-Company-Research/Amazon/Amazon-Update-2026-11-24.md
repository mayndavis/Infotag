# Amazon Update: 24 November 2026

**Coverage window:** 2026-11-23 08:33:54 EAT to 2026-11-24 08:42:29 EAT  
**Primary source:** Amazon company announcement  
**Independent reporting:** Reuters  
**Evidence labels:** Confirmed agreement; company-stated objective; unconfirmed reported transaction value; investor interpretation.

## 1. Agreement to acquire Decart Optimization Stack

Amazon announced on 23 November that it signed an agreement to purchase the proprietary technology behind Decart Optimization Stack (DOS) and bring Decart's co-founders Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev and Orian Leitersdorf, together with its AI researchers and engineers, to Amazon. Amazon says the objective is to improve inference and custom-silicon performance. It says Decart has worked with Amazon since 2025 to improve performance on Trainium. [S10]

The transaction remains subject to customary closing conditions, including regulatory clearance. Amazon's announcement does not disclose consideration. [S10]

Reuters reported that DOS helps AI systems run faster and switch between chips from NVIDIA, Amazon and Google. Reuters also reported that Bloomberg, citing a person familiar with the matter, put the deal value at about $7.5 billion. That figure is **not company-confirmed**. Reuters compared it with Decart's most recent valuation of nearly $4 billion according to PitchBook. [S11]

## 2. Strategic rationale

**Positive:** Optimisation software can increase useful throughput from each accelerator, reduce inference cost per token, improve Trainium utilisation and make AWS custom silicon more attractive to customers. It can therefore improve the return on existing data-centre and silicon investment without relying exclusively on more hardware purchases.

**Negative:** The acquisition's consideration is undisclosed. Integration, retention of key technical staff, product execution and customer adoption remain uncertain. The purchase may fail to deliver expected efficiency gains or could distract engineering resources from Amazon's internal silicon roadmap.

## 3. Capital intensity and investor implications

The financial case depends on measurable improvements in workload performance, utilisation and cost per inference relative to acquisition and integration cost. Without confirmed transaction consideration and post-acquisition performance data, a return-on-invested-capital calculation is not possible.

For the next 1–3 years, monitor Trainium adoption, customer workload migration, inference price/performance, AWS margins and evidence that DOS improves utilisation or lowers cost. Over 3–5 years, successful hardware-software optimisation could strengthen AWS's custom-silicon economics; the acquisition announcement alone does not prove that outcome.
